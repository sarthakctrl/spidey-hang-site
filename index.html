Everything he does

Small app. Lots of web.

A real rope, really swinging

The web is a chain of points with fixed-length links, solved 240 times a second. His ankle is the last point and his head another, so he swings like a double pendulum, and two kinds of grip keep his body in line with the rope.

Drag, fling, nudge, brush

Grab him by the body or anywhere on the rope and throw him. A plain click nudges him away from the click. Sweep the cursor across him without clicking and he gets pushed, like brushing past a hanging charm.

Move the web

Drag the knot along the top of the screen and he swings along behind it. Or pick Left, Center or Right from his menu.

Swing on his own

Keeps a steady swing of about 30 degrees going, pumped like a playground swing. Drop the web too close to an edge and it slides back in just far enough for a full swing.

Breeze

A light, uneven breeze that keeps him swaying. Off by default, so he can go still.

Clicks go straight through

His window is see-through and only catches the mouse over him, the rope and the knot. Everywhere else, your clicks land on whatever is underneath, and grabbing him never takes keyboard focus from the app you are typing in. This page works the same way: select this text right under his rope.

Sleeps when still

When he stops moving the app stops drawing, and uses no CPU until you touch him again. While he swings it draws at most 60 frames a second, and 30 when he only sways on his own.

Reminders in a speech bubble

"Time to drink some water" on every half hour, and "It's your lunch time" at 3:00 PM. The bubble pops up just above his face, types itself out, and follows him as he swings.

Heroes and poses

Seven heroes: six pictures and one drawn in code. Pick one from the menu, or have them change every few minutes. Picture heroes switch between a hanging pose and a swinging pose as he speeds up and slows down. The one hanging here is Iron Spider.

How heroes work

His menu, everywhere

Right-click him, or click the spider-web icon in the menu bar (Windows: by the clock; Ubuntu: the top bar). On Windows a left-click on that icon gives him a swing. The web icon at the top of this page opens the same menu.

A command line

Scripts can talk to a running Spidey: push, reset, swing on, remind, snapshot and more, over a private pipe only your account can open.

Light on top of Electron

About 1.3 MB of Spidey's own code and pictures. The builds are trimmed: English only, maximum compression, one architecture per installer, no tests or tools inside. Electron itself is most of the download.

A web page can't do these, so this site acts them out or just tells you.

On top of every app
The app is a transparent window that stays above everything. Here he only hangs over this page.
Menu bar or tray icon
A spider-web icon in the macOS menu bar, the Windows tray or Ubuntu's top bar. The web icon in this page's top bar stands in for it.
Starts when you sign in
On by default. Windows: a Run entry for your account. macOS: a login item. Linux: an autostart entry. It waits a few seconds for the taskbar or Dock first.
Only one Spidey
Opening the app again while he is up gives him a swing instead of a second Spidey.
Moves himself to Applications
On a Mac, opened from the disk image or Downloads, he offers to move to Applications so starting at login keeps working.
A log file
Every start, layout and problem goes to ~/.spidey-hang/log.txt. Check it first if he misbehaves.

Working out the next reminder…

These are real: leave this page open on the half hour and he will remind you.

  • On the clock, not on a timer. Water comes at :00 and :30 of every hour. Lunch comes at 3:00 PM, and at 3:00 only lunch shows.
  • No stale nagging. If the computer was asleep and a reminder is more than two minutes late, it is skipped.
  • Typed, like a message. The bubble pops in, shows typing dots, types out letter by letter, then stays about 9 seconds. Click it to close it.
  • Always above his face, in every pose, on the side of the web with more room.

Under the hood

See the skeleton.

Switch on the skeleton and swing him: gold dots are the rope's points, blue ones are the web's anchor, his ankle and his head.

  • Verlet rope, 240 steps a second. Speed is just where a point is minus where it was. Links are pulled back to length 64 times every step.
  • Double pendulum. A fixed link from ankle to head, so his body swings on the end of the swinging rope.
  • Two kinds of grip. One damps his body turning faster than the rope's end, the other pulls him back into line. Without them the rope whips like a heavy chain.
  • Settles in about two seconds. Below a slow speed, friction gets much stronger, so the last few pixels of swing don't take a minute to die out.
  • Silk, drawn in layers. A soft shadow, thin strokes side by side for rounded shading, twist marks on top, and a splat of strands at the top of the screen.
  • Reads pixels only when it must. Checking whether the pointer is on him reads the canvas back, so that only happens when the pointer is within reach.

For scripts

Talk to him.

A running Spidey takes one-line commands. This terminal sends them to the Spidey on this page.

spidey.sock

Where the real commands go

Windows
The named pipe \\.\pipe\SpideyHang
macOS
The socket ~/.spidey-hang/spidey.sock, which only your account can open

The app itself also takes --push [n], --reset and --exit on its command line.

snapshot saves only what Spidey himself draws, never the screen behind him.
